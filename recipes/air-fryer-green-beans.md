# Air-Fryer Green Beans
One base method for crisping green beans, then swap the seasoning each night. I worked these out in a convection toaster oven; a basket air fryer runs a bit different — notes below.

## Ingredients

Per portion (scales up — just keep them in a single layer):

<div class="ingredients" markdown="1">
- 100g green beans, trimmed
- 1.5 tsp neutral oil (veg or canola; olive works too)
- salt and pepper
- one of the flavorings below
</div>

<cook-mode-toggle></cook-mode-toggle>

## Steps

### Part 1: Prep ahead
- Trim the beans
- Portion ~100g into containers and refrigerate — they keep about 5 days raw. Use a slightly oversized container so you can rinse and toss in the same one (fewer dishes).

### Part 2: Cook
Convection toaster oven:
- Preheat to 425°F, super convection on
- Rinse the beans and dry them with a towel (you can rinse in the storage container, just dry it after too)
- Toss the dried beans with the oil until lightly glossy (no oil pooling), then add salt, pepper, and any "before" seasonings for your flavoring
- Spread in a single layer with space between them
- Cook ~12 min, until shriveled and browned in spots with a tender-snappy bite
- Add any "after" finish off the heat

Basket air fryer (untested here): same method, but shake once or twice since the beans sit in a solid basket. Air fryers crisp faster and more evenly, so try 400°F and start checking around 8 min.

## Flavorings

Pick one per batch. *Before* = toss on with the oil before cooking. *After* = off the heat once they're done.

### Smoky paprika-cumin — bold, earthy
- **Before:** 1/8 tsp each smoked paprika, garlic powder, ground cumin
- **After (optional):** a squeeze of lime

> Verdict: Amazing, I could eat this every day.

### Lemon-oregano-garlic — bright, herby
- **Before:** 1/8 tsp each garlic powder, onion powder, dried oregano
- **After:** a squeeze of lemon. It relies on the acid, so if it tastes flat add more lemon, not more spice.

> Verdict: Also amazing and I could eat this every day.

### Cumin-lime — warm and tangy (UNTESTED)
- **Before:** 1/8 tsp each ground cumin and garlic powder, plus a lighter pinch of smoked paprika
- **After:** a squeeze of lime

### Soy — mild, savory
- **Before:** oil only, *no salt* (the soy salts it)
- **After:** ~1 tsp soy sauce. For a little sharpness, whisk it with 1/4 tsp mustard powder first.

> Verdict: This was fairly mild but good. In the recipe above I increased the mustard powder to try and make it more interesting.

### Chili-flake — smoky and hot (UNTESTED)
- **Before:** the smoky paprika-cumin mix above
- **Last 2–3 minutes:** a pinch of chili flakes. Add them late or they turn bitter over a full cook.

### Sesame-soy — nutty, savory
- **Before:** oil only, no salt
- **After:** ~1 tsp soy sauce, a drizzle of toasted sesame oil, and a pinch of chili flakes. Sesame oil is a finishing oil — don't cook with it, it burns and turns bitter. Basically restaurant sesame green beans, and a good fix for the plain-soy version tasting flat.

> Verdict: This was much better than soy and mustard! I used Aleppo flakes, but that wasn't spicy enough.

### Harissa — complex, a little spicy
- **Before:** ~1/4 tsp dry harissa blend, tossed on with the oil like the other rubs. It's chili-forward, so if yours runs hot, add it partway through or keep the cook shorter — ground chilies turn bitter over a long, hot cook. Caraway and coriander give it a different character from the smoky rub.

> Verdict: I did a heaping 1/4 tsp and it wasn't quite flavorful enough for me. Next time I'll try more.

## Tips
- Don't crowd them — crowded beans steam instead of blister, so two rounds beats one packed layer.
- Store raw, not cooked. Crisped beans go soft in the fridge, so portion them raw and crisp a fresh batch each time. That's the trick to having them most nights without much work.
- Before vs. after: dry spices and garlic/onion powder go on before, with the oil. Anything acidic, liquid, or delicate — lemon/lime, soy, chili flakes, fresh herbs — goes on late or after, or it burns.
- Salt before is fine and spreads more evenly. The exception is the soy finishes, since the soy's already salty.
- Oil: neutral oil handles the heat best. Olive usually works; if it smokes or tastes bitter, switch oils or drop to 375°F.
